# Carrier (mp_carrier): the owner's target look (2026-10-24)

`target_overview.png`, `target_deck.png`, `target_interior.png`, `target_crew_room.png`, `target_stairs.png`, `target_hangar_bay.png`, `target_bow_island.png` and `target_flight_deck.png` are the look the owner wants for
Carrier, not pictures of Black Ops II's own map. What they ask of the frame:

- Late sun low on the horizon, warm key light and long shadows; a stormy,
  layered cloud sky with the sun breaking through.
- A wet deck: bright sun specular streaks on the deck plates and puddles.
- Wrecked jets burning on the deck, with tall black smoke columns.
- A rough dark sea with whitecaps and a bright sun glitter path.
- Small warm and red lights on the island and deck structures, glowing in
  the dusk.
- Fog thickening toward the horizon, and strong contrast between the sunlit
  deck and the shaded structures.

`target_interior.png` is the below-deck look (the interior spaces):
- Dusk-dim, mostly ambient light; dark gunmetal walls and ceiling.

- Wet, puddled dark steel deck with soft specular reflections of the
  lights; riveted, weathered plates and scuffs.
- Coloured pipe runs (red, yellow, blue) along the ceiling; caged
  bulkhead lamps and ceiling lights as small warm pools of light.
- Electrical panels with small amber and blue indicator lights, red valve
  wheels, hose reels on a red-striped base.
- Strong contrast, slight cool-blue shadow tint, warm orange accents.

Note (owner, 2026-10-24): these pictures were generated by an image model
as Black Ops III styled renders of the carrier. They set the mood, light,
colour and materials, not the exact layout: where a picture shows
something that does not belong (the second fire by the crane in
`target_flight_deck.png`), the map's own layout wins and the thing is left
out.

Priority (owner, 2026-10-24): the map's layout must match the real map
(Carrier's own geometry, routes, cover and sightlines) and is the most
important thing. The look is applied on top of it. Nothing is moved,
added or removed in the layout to copy a picture; a picture is matched
only in light, colour, materials, weather and the effects that fit.

Judgement (owner, 2026-10-24): the pictures cannot cover every angle of
the map. Where there is no picture for a place, use common sense: carry
the same light, colour, wear, wetness and mood into it, in the spirit of
the pictures, rather than leaving it as it was or inventing something
that clashes.

Decision (owner, 2026-10-24): Carrier is to look like these pictures, and
only like them. This look replaces BO2's clear daytime lighting on Carrier,
with no switch back to the original: sun, sky, fog, grade, the wet deck,
the burning wrecks and the ocean.

## Implementation status (2026-10-27)

Pass 1 (lighting, sky, fog, sea, wetness) is in code, untested on a GPU:
`crates/assets/src/lane/carrier_look.rs`, forced on for `mp_carrier` through
the Black Ops III look (`bo3_look`); `BO2MP_CARRIER_LOOK=0` is a test aid only.

- Sun: the map's yaw, elevation 9 degrees, warm (1, 0.56, 0.30) at the original
  luminance, lifted up to 2.2x for the lost N.L; used for the frame's light, the
  water and the baked sun shadow map (long shadows).
- Fog: cool grey-blue (0.42, 0.47, 0.55) / warm (1, 0.62, 0.35) toward the sun
  at the original luminance, half distance x0.6, fog banks retinted, fog
  modifier volumes dropped.
- Look row (light row 251, vec4s 1004..1007; the shader's `look_on`/`look_row`):
  wetness and puddles (value noise on world xy; up-facing surfaces only; darker
  albedo, gloss raised, puddles flat and near-mirror, f0 0.03), the ambient
  tint and scale, the sky grade (greyed and darkened overhead, procedural
  broken clouds thinned near the sun, lit cloud edges), the warm sun glow low.
- Sea: the open-sea water material's colours scaled darker and cooler
  (`BO2MP_CARRIER_SEA`); the glitter follows the new sun.
- Exposure 1.3 (`BO2MP_CARRIER_EXPOSURE`).
- Tunables: see the README entry of 2026-10-27.

Pass 2 (not done): texture swaps from other games (deck plates, wet and worn
paint), the sky picture itself (a real stormy dusk cube instead of the grade
and noise clouds, which also removes the old painted sun), cloud shadows,
red/amber small lights on the island and deck edges, rain-wet normal ripples
in puddles, the below-deck interiors' own pass (pipes, caged lamps, porthole
glow), the sea's whitecaps and foam, checking the map's fire and smoke effects
play, and tuning every number against these pictures on PC.
